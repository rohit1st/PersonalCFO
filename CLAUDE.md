# Net Worth Planner — project context for Claude

Read this first in every session. It carries over the decisions made while building the app in a claude.ai chat (Personal CFO project), so work can continue in Cowork or Claude Code without re-explaining.

## What it is

A private, offline net worth and retirement planner for friends and family: "Your confidential companion 🤫 You can run me without wifi." Static site (no server, no build step, no dependencies) hosted on GitHub Pages and installable as a PWA. People answer a short quick start, then refine inputs; the app projects net worth year by year with best / mid / worst cases from a 1,000-path Monte Carlo simulation and headlines a **work-optional age** (first key result tile).

Owner: the user (admin). They edit `config.js` for defaults and the example household.

## Non-negotiables

1. **Privacy.** Nothing a user enters ever leaves their device. Data is saved in the browser's localStorage only. The page's CSP includes `connect-src 'none'`; never add network calls, analytics, CDNs, external fonts or images.
2. **Offline.** Everything must work without wifi after first load (service worker caches app files).
3. **Not advice.** Keep the educational framing and disclaimer. Terms of use live in `config.js`; users must tick an acceptance box on the quick start welcome screen. Don't write copy that sounds like personalized financial advice.
4. **Design system.** Pastel, light, uncluttered. Off-white background, one calm accent from the chosen theme (default Linen: espresso `--teal-deep #6B5137`), green = good, coral = bad, amber = caution. **No purple anywhere.** Rounded cards, soft shadows, system font stack. Every screen must work on phones (test at 360–375px wide).
5. **Run the evals** (`node evals/run.js`) after any change to `model.js` or `config.js`, and don't update golden results unless the change was intended.

## How the model works (model.js)

- Year 0 = today's balances; each later row is a full year. Balances grow by that year's return, then the year's flows apply at year end.
- Returns are lognormal: `exp(ln(1 + r) + vol·z)`, one shared market shock per year for all investment accounts, fixed random seed (`mulberry32(20240611)`), 1,000 paths. Entered return = median growth. Best/mid/worst = 90th/50th/10th percentile per year.
- Balances: pre-tax retirement per person (R1, R2; joint pre-tax split evenly, all Partner 1's if single), Roth (Q), non-retirement (N), home and other (O, never sold).
- Income is **before tax** (`incomeBasis: 'gross'`). Taxes are estimated per year in today's dollars: federal brackets + standard deduction (single/joint by partner toggle), Social Security (wage base) + Medicare + additional Medicare, flat state rate. Pre-tax contributions reduce taxable income; Roth ones don't. Income types: salary, `benefit` (Social Security: taxable share from the IRS provisional-income formula, 0–85%, thresholds `benefitThresholds` fixed in law so they shrink in today's dollars; no state tax unless `taxes.stateTaxesSocialSecurity`), `pension` (pension or annuity: fully taxable, federal and state), other. People 65+ (still in the plan) get `extraDeduction65` each, plus `seniorDeduction` ($6,000 each through 2028, minus 6% of income over $75k/$150k). Optional flat override (`payTaxRate`; flat rate × pay, other, pensions and taxable Social Security). Plans saved before this used take-home pay (`incomeBasis: 'net'`): no tax applied, and the UI offers "Switch to pay before taxes".
- Cash flow per year: income − taxes − your contributions − spending − loan payments − college/purchases. Surplus goes to non-retirement. Shortfall draws from non-retirement, then pre-tax (enough extra to cover its tax), then Roth (tax-free); if all are empty, savings "ran out" that year.
- **RMDs**: from 73 (75 if born 1960+, birth year = this year − age), each year's minimum = last year-end pre-tax balance ÷ IRS Uniform Lifetime Table divisor (`requiredWithdrawals` in config). Taken after growth, taxed with the year's other income (below), applied to the year's cash flow before any other withdrawal; surplus goes to non-retirement. After one partner's plan-until age, the other takes RMDs on their accounts at their own age. No still-working exception. Result: `rmd` (mid case, nominal) and `rmdAges`.
- **Tax on pre-tax withdrawals** (RMDs and shortfall withdrawals): by default the extra income tax from adding them to that year's other income (`incomeTax()`: brackets, more Social Security becoming taxable, 65+ deductions, state rate), solved per path so the withdrawal covers its own tax (`grossUp`). `withdrawalTaxRate` (blank = estimate) is a flat override. The old `withdrawalTax` key from plans saved before v15 is dropped on load. Roth and non-retirement withdrawals are untaxed. Result: `taxes` (tax on income, same in every path) and `wtax` (tax on withdrawals, mid case); Year by year shows their sum.
- Salaries grow at salary growth and always stop at the owner's retirement age (a salary end age can only stop them sooner; work after retiring = Other income); everything else entered in today's dollars rises with inflation (college with college inflation), except non-salary income with `cola: false` (`incomeGrows()`: Social Security and other income rise unless set off; pensions don't unless set on), which stays at the same dollar amount. Contributions stop when the owner retires (joint when both have).
- Loans amortize monthly; if only a paid-off year is given, the payment is calculated; if both are given, any remainder is paid as a lump sum in that year. A loan with `startYear` begins at the end of that year: its balance is in today's dollars (inflated to then), payments start the next year and amortize to `endYear`.
- Home and other assets can have `fromYear` (bought later: today's-dollar price inflated to that year, then home growth) and `sellYear` (its grown value moves into non-retirement savings that year). These are deterministic.
- **Life ideas** (`IDEAS`, `ideaDefaults(plan, id)`, `applyIdea(plan, id, params)`, `ideaLabel`): kid, home, move, break, downsize, family, windfall, purchase. Each writes ordinary plan items (dependents with negative age = not born yet, spending windows, purchases, future loans/homes, a split salary for a career break, a negative purchase for an untaxed windfall), never mutates its input, and stacks. Suggested values come from the plan and `lifeIdeas` in config.js.
- Spending items: `always`, `pre` (before everyone has retired), `ret`, or `ages` (Partner 1's ages).
- **Moving retirement** (`shiftRetirement(plan, years)`): both partners move by the same years, and so does the end age of each person's last salary (latest start; ties → latest end), for Coast FIRE plans. Earlier salaries keep their end ages. Used by the Play with it slider/presets and by work-optional; editing "Retire at" in the plan does not shift salaries.
- **Work-optional age**: earliest Partner 1 retirement age (moved with `shiftRetirement`) where savings last the whole plan in ≥ 85% of paths (`workOptionalConfidence` in config). Binary search using 400-path runs.

## UI conventions and decisions (keep these)

- App name "Net Worth Planner"; headings "Your plan" (left) and "Your Projections" (right) sit at the same height above their cards; toolbar icons (Open, Save a copy, Export, Erase) next to "Your plan" (no Reset: Erase or "Start your own plan" covers starting over); a **Settings** button top right of the header (icon only on phones) opens a right-side drawer (`<dialog id="settings">`): Your plan (Save a copy, Load a backup, Export projections, Erase my data), This app (Add to home screen, Share with friends, Refresh app + version), Look and feel (theme swatches), and a privacy line; sharing lives in the footer line "Built with love ❤️ for friends and family. Click to share" with a share-arrow icon; its "Link copied ✓" message shows under that line, not at the top (app link only, never plan data: share sheet on touch devices, falling back to copy unless they closed it; copy + "Link copied ✓" on desktop; a copy-the-link dialog if the clipboard is blocked); header, banners and columns share one width (1360px); Today's/Future dollars toggle inside the chart card, next to the chart legend.
- Left column is a full-height sticky sidebar on desktop; right column, top to bottom: the four key result tiles, chart card (legend + dollars toggle, chart, Play with it sliders, one summary sentence, "How is this calculated?" link to How the projection works), Scenarios, Year by year, How the projection works. Keep simulation details (1,000 paths, percentiles, run time) in How the projection works, not under the chart.
- Play with it slider labels: retire at "62"; spending "$219k/year (+10%)"; returns "5.5% (−1 pt), ≈3% after inflation"; inflation "3% (+0.5 pts)" (with an investment mix, returns follow inflation). Sliders only, no preset chips; two columns on desktop.
- Scenario table shows mid-case figures only, with a caption "Mid case, in today's dollars"; the first row is "Your plan" (the saved-file format still labels it "(current inputs)"); eye toggle and trash icons (delete has Undo). No "Load" action.
- Chart milestones: life events (college 🎓, loans paid off ✅/🏡, benefits 💵), work-optional 🎉, and `milestones()` from model.js: net worth round numbers in today's dollars 💰 (at most four) and the 4% rule 🏁 (mid-case savings, not home, × 4% ≥ that year's spending + loan payments). They show as icons on the chart and in the hover tip (no pills under the chart).
- Color themes: `themes` in config.js: Linen (default), Harbor, Rosewood, Fjord (Lagoon and Graphite were retired in v15; a saved id that no longer exists falls back to the first). Each theme has colors bg, surface, well, line, ink, muted and the accent family accent/accentSoft/accentDeep/accentHover/accentSofter/accentSoftHover plus heroA/heroB). The page maps them onto the CSS variables (`--teal*` are the accent family, kept for history), saves the choice as `netWorthPlanner.theme` (+ `themeVars`, applied by an inline head script before first paint), and cross-fades on switch (none with reduced motion). Themes change neutrals and the accent only: green / coral / amber stay semantic. `evals/suites/09-themes.js` enforces AA contrast, light backgrounds and no purple.
- Erase my data (toolbar + Settings): confirmation dialog with "Save a copy first", removes every `netWorthPlanner.*` localStorage key and stops autosave, then an "All clear" screen (Close tries window.close; Start fresh reloads).
- On phones, "Year by year" starts collapsed and uses the same left-caret collapse control as the input sections.
- Assumptions: returns are stored before inflation (the model's inputs), but each return field shows the after-inflation rate, with an amber note outside `returnAfterInflationNote`. The Investment mix picker (presets in config `investmentMixes`, defined after inflation) fills both returns and ups and downs; `assumptions.mix` holds the choice ('custom' or missing = hand-set). Changing inflation re-derives preset returns; editing a return or ups and downs switches to Custom. Helpers `realRate`, `nominalRate`, `mixAssumptions` live in model.js.
- Key result tiles above the chart (`keyResults()` in model.js), four of them: (1) work-optional age, the headline, in the green gradient tile with 🎉, number reveal, confetti and Share (retired: "You're retired 🌴" + chance savings last; never: "80+"); (2) savings last X% of markets + worst case (retired: tough markets); (3) net worth at retirement (or in 10 years if retired); (4) first-year draw from savings (tone: ≤4% green, ≤5% amber; net worth at plan end if there's no retirement year). Two columns on phones.
- Life ideas in Scenarios: a grid of 8 idea buttons (drawn icons) above the manual "save your plan as it is now" row. Picking one opens an inline composer (not a modal): stacked idea chips, the idea as a sentence with inline inputs (punctuation kept with its value), "where these come from" notes, a live result (work-optional, savings last, net worth at plan end, with deltas) and a "Life idea" line on the chart (replaces the what-if line while open), an "In short" line (`ideaSummary(plan, id, params)` in model.js: one plain sentence with amount, Partner 1's age and year, e.g. "Give family $100k at age 50 (2036)"), then Save as scenario (auto name from `ideaLabel(id, params, plan)` with ages; the summaries are saved as the scenario's `about`, shown in a full-width line under its row and at the top of its details, and kept in backups as `_about` rows; opens its details) / Apply to my plan (Undo) / + Add another idea / Cancel.
- Scenario details: tapping a saved scenario's name expands a row listing what differs from the current plan (`planDiff()` in model.js: sections, plain labels, before → after, items added/removed by position) and a current → scenario comparison of work-optional age, chance savings last, net worth at plan end and worst case.
- "What's new": `whatsNew` in config.js (newest first, one per release, ≤160 characters, playful). Shown once at the top to people who had a saved plan from an earlier version; `netWorthPlanner.seenVersion` in localStorage remembers the last one seen. New visitors don't see it.
- Updates (sw.js + the registration code at the end of index.html): each version installs every app file fresh (`cache: 'reload'`) into its own cache and serves only from it (cache first, no background refresh, so versions never mix). The page registers with `updateViaCache: 'none'`, applies a waiting update on open or when it becomes visible again, checks `reg.update()` on visibility and hourly, and otherwise shows a sticky "Update now". Footer: "Version vN" (from `whatsNew[0]`) and a Settings link; **Refresh app** lives in Settings (unregisters the worker, deletes `net-worth-planner-*` caches, reloads; localStorage untouched).
- Number entry: percent fields use `inputmode="decimal"`, accept "," as the decimal point, and have − / + stepper buttons (0.25; 1 for ups and downs; none on Taxes on pay or Tax on withdrawals, which are blank = auto) because some phone keyboards show no decimal point; money fields treat "," as thousands.
- Names flow everywhere: renaming a person updates labels like "Sam's 401(k)".
- Quick start ("Step N of 7" beside the progress bar on the seven question screens): welcome (privacy note, disclaimer, accept checkbox, example / open links) → people → retire age → kids → savings → home → pay before tax + your contributions + employer → spending (pre-filled guess). Guesses are tagged "Estimated" until edited. A plan-detail checklist in "Your plan" guides refinement.
- Income summary: before tax, taxes (%), "Into retirement from pay", take-home, then a "Total saving" line (contributions + employer + what's left after spending and loan payments; college and big purchases excluded because they're paid from savings).
- Footer: "Built with love ❤️" + short disclaimer + Terms of use link. No contact email.
- Copy style: plain, friendly, sentence case, no jargon without explanation; info "i" tooltips for concepts like market ups and downs and taxes.

## Working agreements

- Before changing the math: write or update an eval first, run `node evals/run.js`, then change the code.
- After editing `config.js`: `node evals/sync-builtin.js` (keeps model.js's fallback copy in sync), then `node evals/run.js`. Example-household golden results will move; update them with `--update` if intended.
- Before shipping: evals pass; test the page at desktop and ~375px; check the browser console is clean; bump `VERSION` in `sw.js`; add a line to `CHANGELOG.md`; add a matching `whatsNew` note in `config.js` (the evals check it).
- Test locally with `python3 -m http.server 8000` in this folder (service worker and install need http, not file://).
- Keep everything dependency-free and in these few files unless there's a strong reason.

## Known limitations / ideas backlog

- Taxes: no itemized deductions, credits, capital gains on brokerage withdrawals, IRMAA, state brackets or the 10% early-withdrawal penalty before 59½; filing stays joint after one partner's plan ends (no widow's tax jump); the additional Medicare threshold is treated as rising with inflation (it doesn't).
- Returns are independent year to year (no mean reversion); defaults are conservative (4% rule ≈ 77% success vs ~90–95% historically).
- Spending doesn't change if a partner dies; Social Security guesses in quick start are very rough (labelled as such in quick start and under Income).
- No sync between devices (by design). Idea: QR-code transfer between phone and laptop.
- Ideas raised but not built: shareable milestone card exists; could add plan-completeness rewards, Personal CFO modules (CSV imports, expenses, marketplace) from the PRD in the chat project.
